Sitemap and Robots.txt Checker Guide

Understand the different jobs of XML sitemaps and robots.txt, and how to check that they are accessible.

Open Sitemap & Robots Checker →

What this tool does

An XML sitemap lists canonical URLs that a site owner wants search engines to discover. Robots.txt gives crawlers instructions about paths they may request. They are not interchangeable.

How to use it

Enter your domain and check the sitemap and robots.txt URLs. Open each file in a browser, confirm the sitemap contains canonical live URLs, and ensure robots rules do not unintentionally block important content.

Practical tips and best practices

Keep the sitemap updated, include only preferred indexable URLs, use absolute URLs and submit it through the relevant search engine webmaster tools. Review robots.txt carefully before publishing changes.

Important limitations

Robots.txt is not a reliable way to keep private information out of search. Use authentication or access controls for private content.

Frequently asked questions

Does a sitemap guarantee indexing?

No. It helps discovery but does not guarantee crawling or indexing.

Can robots.txt remove a page from search?

Not reliably. Blocking crawling may prevent a crawler from seeing a noindex directive.

Should every URL be in the sitemap?

Include canonical, useful URLs intended for indexing.

Where should robots.txt live?

At the root of the host, for example example.com/robots.txt.

What if the checker cannot fetch a file?

Check the URL, hosting response, redirects and access restrictions.
